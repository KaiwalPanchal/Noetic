# Philosophy

## Thinking in frameworks is the most underrated skill of the AI age.

You've heard a few of them already. Keep it stupid simple. Thinking, fast and slow. Steal like an artist. One-way and two-way doors.

Most people hear these once, nod, and move on. That's the mistake.

A framework is someone else's years of thinking, compressed into something you can run. You can carry it into a decision, a design, a piece of writing, a codebase. A quote just sits there. A framework does work.

The people who think in frameworks don't have better ideas. They have better tools for working on their ideas.

## AI moved the bottleneck.

Learning from a book used to mean reading it twice, taking notes, and hoping some of it showed up when you needed it. Mostly it didn't.

Now an agent can read the source, pull out *how the author thinks*, and keep it next to your work, ready to apply. Consuming and understanding content is basically solved.

What's left is **taste**: knowing what to keep, what to reject, and what you're actually trying to build. That part is yours, and it's the only part that matters.

So here's my stance: **everyone who builds anything should be running a harness like this.** I don't mean a chatbot you ask questions. I mean a system that holds your frameworks and your taste, and puts them to work on what you're making.

## OverMind is that harness.

I'm presenting OverMind as a framework in its own right. It's four blocks: projects, knowledge, tools, actions. You wire them in whatever order your work needs. It's modular on purpose, because creative work never runs in a straight line.

Three things it's built for:

1. **Organising what you consume.** Sources in, frameworks out. Not summaries. A summary is dead weight. A framework is a tool.
2. **Managing your chain of thought.** The ideas you've had, the stances you hold, the things you reject. All on disk, connected, so the next idea starts from everything before it and not from zero.
3. **Loops that make an idea the best version of itself.** Draft, critique, revise, repeat. The system runs the loop. You decide what survives.

## Why loops: evolution.

The more you iterate on something, the faster you get to good. This isn't a productivity tip. It's the one rule every system that gets better follows.

- **Nature:** variation, selection, repeat. Millions of generations, no designer.
- **Machine learning:** guess, measure the loss, adjust, repeat. A model is just the result of enough iterations.
- **Learning a skill:** reps with feedback. The person who does 100 bad attempts beats the one planning the perfect first one.

Same shape every time: try, get feedback, keep what works, go again. The quality of your first attempt barely matters. The number of iterations and the speed of feedback decide everything.

So OverMind optimises for cycle time, not for first-try perfection. The machine generates the variations. **You are the selection pressure.** Your taste is what decides which version survives into the next round.

## What I believe

- Frameworks over summaries. If you can't execute it, you didn't learn it.
- Taste is subtraction. What you reject defines you more than what you collect.
- Iteration count beats first-draft quality. Every time.
- The machine drafts, you decide. Taste can't be outsourced, and it shouldn't be.
- Iterate fast on hats, slow down on tattoos. Not every decision deserves the same loop.
- Ship over structure. Organising your second brain isn't output. Shipped work is.
